The shape of the problem: p99 goes 180ms → 2.4s roughly 14:00–16:00 Tuesdays, p50 barely moves. App tier CPU and memory are flat, so it's not your service doing the work. The database shows matching lock wait time, so the queue is there.

The suspect: `config/cron.yaml` has a `reports` job scheduled `0 14 * * 2` — Tuesday at 14:00 — running a monthly rollup. Start time matches the window exactly. A rollup job holding long transactions or table locks would produce exactly this: fine median, ugly tail, flat app CPU, rising DB lock waits.

The evidence that makes me more confident: it happened 6 of the last 8 Tuesdays, and both clean Tuesdays were public holidays. If the cron fires unconditionally, holidays shouldn't matter — which either means the job is skipped/short-circuited on holidays, or the traffic that collides with it isn't there. Either way, holiday-sensitivity is consistent with a contention story, not a "cron is inherently slow" story.

What I can't tell you: I can't see what queries the job issues, and I don't have query-level DB data for the window. So I cannot confirm this. It's a strong correlation with a plausible mechanism, not a diagnosis. Two things would embarrass this theory — if the job actually finishes in 30 seconds, or if lock waits are on tables the rollup never touches.

To confirm or kill it, cheapest first:

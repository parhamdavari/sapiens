Yes, I'd move — but the version of the move I'd recommend is smaller and more boring than what your team is probably arguing about.

Here's how I read the situation. The pain you described is real and it's the expensive kind: two days of calendar time and five PRs to make one logical change. That's not just slow, it's a tax that changes behavior. People stop making small improvements to shared libraries because the ceremony costs more than the change. That compounds quietly for years.

The CI objection, on the other hand, is unmeasured — and in the form it's usually made, it's wrong. A monorepo doesn't mean you build everything on every commit. With path filters, a PR that touches one service builds that one service, and you're back to roughly your current 6 minutes. The case where a monorepo genuinely costs more CI is a shared-library change that fans out to all four services — but that's exactly the case where you *want* to build all four, and today you're paying for it anyway, just spread over two days and four PRs instead of one pipeline run.

So the real risk isn't build time. It's that nobody there has run a monorepo, you have no platform engineer, and monorepo tooling is a genuine rabbit hole. That risk is manageable if you stay disciplined about scope:

- One repo, one CI config, path-filtered jobs. That's it.
- No Nx, no Turborepo, no Bazel, no remote cache on day one. At 7 projects, plain path filters are sufficient. Add tooling when you can point at the specific problem it solves.
- Kill the private registry publish step for internal consumers. Direct workspace references. That's where the two days goes.

Two other things I'd suggest, both about people rather than tooling:

Have the engineer who's against it own the CI setup. Not as a punishment — he's the one with the sharpest instinct for where it'll degrade, and that's the person you want designing the thing. It also converts the loudest objection into shared ownership instead of a standing grudge.

And before you start, spend an afternoon measuring: how long does a full four-service build actually take today if you run them in parallel? Put a number on it. Right now the debate is two opinions and no data, which is why it keeps looping.

If for some reason the team can't get to consensus, there's a cheaper intermediate: keep the repos, but automate the fan-out with Changesets plus auto-merge on green. That gets you from two days to a few hours for maybe a week of work. It's a real option, not a consolation prize — but it doesn't fix the underlying structure, and you'll likely be having this conversation again in a year.
